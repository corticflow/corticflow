<p>When hundreds of thousands of festival-goers descend upon a concentrated geographic location, traditional mobile networks often crumble under the weight of simultaneous live streams, messaging, and navigation queries. Beyond consumer connectivity, high-density crowds present a massive logistical challenge for city managers and emergency services attempting to monitor transit corridors, control bottlenecks, and deploy public safety resources in real time.</p><p>For the upcoming Rock in Rio festival, Italian-owned Brazilian carrier TIM and the Rio de Janeiro Operations Center (COR-Rio) have unveiled a sophisticated smart-city partnership. By leveraging aggregated, anonymized mobility telemetry from 4G and 5G networks, the joint initiative will construct dynamic heatmaps to track human displacement from initial transit points all the way into the venue. Combining these spatial analytics with 5G Advanced (5.5G) capabilities and network slicing, the project serves as a live laboratory for the future of urban intelligence and event management.</p><h2>Real-Time Heatmaps and Spatial Telemetry</h2><p>At the core of the agreement between TIM and COR-Rio is the continuous processing of mobile network signaling data. As mobile devices connect to cell towers across the city, network logs generate geospatial data points. When aggregated and stripped of personally identifiable information (PII), this metadata offers an exceptionally granular view of population movement.</p><p>The system will refresh every 30 minutes, providing municipal operators at COR-Rio with updated heatmaps that illustrate human concentration and movement vectors. This capability enables authorities to analyze not just the density inside the festival grounds (Cidade do Rock), but the entire macro-journey—monitoring arterial highways, bus rapid transit (BRT) routes, and arrival corridors long before attendees reach the gates.</p><blockquote><p>"At Rock in Rio, we provide heatmaps so that the Rio Operations Center can monitor public concentration and displacement along the route," explained Marco Di Costanzo, Vice President of Technological Development at TIM. "The information is updated every 30 minutes. The data is anonymized, aggregated, and strictly non-individualized."</p></blockquote><p>These predictive flow analytics will also feed into post-event mobility studies, helping urban planners optimize mass transit routing, exit strategies, and emergency access lanes for future large-scale events in the metropolitan region.</p><h2>Infrastructure Blueprint: 5G Advanced and Smart Antenna Arrays</h2><p>Managing network demand for an estimated 130,000 daily visitors—where approximately 60% of all mobile traffic is projected to traverse 5G networks—requires a substantial overhaul of physical and logical infrastructure. To handle the expected surge, TIM is deploying a specialized setup featuring more than 120 intelligent, high-capacity antennas across the festival footprint.</p><p>These smart antennas offer up to 50% more capacity and expand coverage range by 30% compared to conventional mobile solutions. Crucially, the layout of the physical radio access network (RAN) has been mapped directly against the venue’s architectural modifications.</p><blockquote><p>"One of the key inputs for antenna allocation planning is the physical layout within Cidade do Rock. The Espaço Favela expanded this year, so we allocated a higher density of antennas to that region," noted Homero Salum, Director of Engineering at TIM. "Rock in Rio also created two designated comfort zones, which received special attention in our engineering design."</p></blockquote><p>In tandem with physical antenna positioning, TIM is integrating 5G Advanced (Release 18 and beyond) capabilities alongside automated network management systems. These automated controllers dynamically adjust capacity allocation across different sectors of the venue in real time, reacting instantaneously to localized traffic spikes—such as when a headliner finishes a performance on the main stage and thousands of users simultaneously attempt to send video content or call rideshare services.</p><h2>Mission-Critical Reliability via Network Slicing</h2><p>One of the most technically compelling aspects of the deployment is a real-world pilot of network slicing for operational security and staff communications. In traditional mobile network architectures, operational teams share the same spectral resources as the general public, creating a risk that severe consumer network congestion could block critical voice and data transmissions for event staff.</p><p>Through 5G network slicing, TIM creates a virtually isolated, logical sub-network dedicated exclusively to Push-to-Talk (PTT) radio communications for event operators and security personnel. By guaranteeing dedicated bandwidth and ultra-low latency latency bounds for this operational slice, mission-critical communications remain completely immune to public data spikes, regardless of overall network load.</p><h2>The Broader Telecom and Urban Infrastructure Paradigm</h2><p>During the 2024 edition of Rock in Rio, TIM alone processed over 185 terabytes of data across its network. As consumption patterns shift increasingly toward high-bitrate video uploads, spatial computing assets, and live streaming, the network load at future events will scale exponentially.</p><p>The partnership between TIM and COR-Rio demonstrates how mobile network operators (MNOs) are evolving beyond passive utility providers into core infrastructure partners for smart cities. By converting raw network telemetry into actionable urban intelligence while implementing advanced 5G features like slicing and automated beamforming, telecom carriers are defining the operational playbook for hyper-connected smart cities worldwide.</p>
