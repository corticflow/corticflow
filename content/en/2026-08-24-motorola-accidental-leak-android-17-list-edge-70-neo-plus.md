In what appears to be an unforced administrative slip, Lenovo and its subsidiary Motorola have accidentally disclosed the existence of two previously unannounced smartphones. An official update eligibility list published by the tech giant outlining devices slated to receive Android 17 prominently features the unreleased Motorola Edge 70 Neo and Motorola Edge 70 Plus. While software roadmaps are usually closely guarded until hardware is formally announced, this official document provides definitive proof that Motorola is already laying the groundwork for its next-generation mid-range and upper-mid-range portfolio.

However, the document has sparked just as much intrigue for what it omits as what it includes. Alongside the premature debut of the Edge 70 series variants, the published list inexplicably excludes several recently launched smartphones that, according to Motorola's own official update commitments, ought to be fully eligible for Android 17. The resulting confusion highlights both the ongoing complexity of Android software maintenance and potential disorganization within Lenovo's documentation pipeline.

## Unveiling the Next Generation: Edge 70 Neo and Edge 70 Plus

While Motorola has not released hardware specifications, render images, or launch dates for the new models, their inclusion in an official Android 17 deployment manifest confirms two key strategic directions for the brand:

1. **Guaranteed Out-of-the-Box Software Roadmap:** Both the Edge 70 Neo and Edge 70 Plus are confirmed to be part of Motorola’s active long-term update strategy, implying they will launch either with Android 15 or Android 16 and receive guaranteed platform upgrades through Android 17.
2. **Expansion of the Edge Tier Structure:** The naming convention fits directly into Motorola’s established product segmentation. The Edge 70 Neo will likely serve as a compact, design-focused upper-mid-range option balancing price and lifestyle features, whereas the Edge 70 Plus is poised to sit directly beneath the flagship 'Pro' or 'Ultra' variants, featuring enhanced processing power, larger displays, and upgraded camera arrays.

By inadvertently revealing these designations, Motorola underscores that its Edge product strategy remains aggressively focused on filling every price point between mainstream mid-rangers and premium flagships.

## Perplexing Omissions and Documentation Flaws

The most puzzling aspect of the published document lies in the notable absence of several prominent 2024 and 2025 devices. Multiple handsets that were explicitly sold with promises of three to five major OS updates were entirely left off the Android 17 list.

Key missing models include:
- **Motorola Edge 50 Series:** The standard Edge 50, Edge 50 Pro, Edge 50 Neo, and Edge 50 Fusion—all debuted in 2024 running Android 14—are entirely absent from the current matrix. Given that Motorola explicitly pledged up to five OS updates for models like the Edge 50 Neo, omitting them from an Android 17 list is a striking anomaly.
- **Motorola Edge 60 Neo:** Launched in 2025 with Android 15 and a five-year OS support pledge, its absence raises immediate questions.
- **Razr 2024:** The company's mainstream foldable, launched with Android 14 and guaranteed three OS upgrades, is also missing.
- **Moto G Series:** Popular value devices like the Moto G56 5G (8GB RAM variant) and Moto G75 5G, which carried multi-year update guarantees, fail to appear on the published document.

Adding to the evidence that the document suffered from hasty editorial compilation, the **Motorola Edge 60** appears twice in the table. This duplicate entry strongly suggests that a copy-paste error occurred during drafting, where a missing entry—such as an Edge 50 variant—was accidentally overwritten with duplicate text.

## Software Support as a Competitive Battleground

Software support longevity has become one of the primary decision-making factors for smartphone buyers. With Google and Samsung setting industry standards by pledging seven full years of OS and security updates on flagship devices—and extending multi-year commitments to budget lines—Motorola has faced mounting pressure to elevate its post-purchase software commitment.

Historically, Motorola faced criticism for sluggish update rollouts and limited OS upgrade cycles. To counter this reputation, the company significantly revised its policy across recent generations, guaranteeing extended support for the Edge 50 and Edge 60 series. Accidental document blunders like this one, while likely harmless editorial errors, can inadvertently create customer anxiety regarding whether software promises will be honored.

Industry analysts expect Lenovo to issue a corrected document in the coming days, reinstating the missing Edge 50, Edge 60, and Razr models once administrative errors are resolved. Meanwhile, mobile enthusiasts will be keeping a close eye on upcoming trade shows and announcement windows, where the Motorola Edge 70 Neo and Edge 70 Plus are now officially expected to make their formal debut.
