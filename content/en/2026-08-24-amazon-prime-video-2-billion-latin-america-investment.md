title: "Amazon's $2 Billion Latin America Gambit: How Prime Video Is Redefining Streaming Strategy in Emerging Markets"
date: "2026-08-24"
category: "Business & Startups"
---

In what represents the largest regional financial commitment in its history, Amazon has officially confirmed that Prime Video will inject over **$2 billion (approximately R$ 10.3 billion)** into Latin America between 2027 and 2030. Unveiled during the inaugural *Prime Video Presents Latin America* showcase in Mexico City, the multi-billion-dollar initiative signals a dramatic escalation in Big Tech’s battle for entertainment dominance across emerging markets.

The massive capital deployment isn't merely focused on content creation; it spans local original productions, high-profile licensed acquisitions, live sports broadcasting, and platform infrastructure expansion across five key regional anchors: Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, and Chile. Kelly Day, Vice President of Prime Video International, emphasized that this capital infusion reflects a broad-based, multi-genre strategy designed to capture diverse consumer behaviors across the entire territory.

### Doubling Down on Hyper-Local Storytelling

Central to Amazon’s announcement is the target to **more than double the output of Local Originals** across these five nations by 2027 compared to 2026 benchmarks. The company revealed a slate of over 25 new regional titles slated for 2027, spanning scripted drama, feature films, unscripted documentaries, and reality television formats.

In Brazil—Latin America's largest media market—Amazon is deploying a slate heavy on cultural resonance and mainstream appeal. Upward projections include the second season of true-crime phenomenon *Tremembé*, a comprehensive documentary series on late country legend Marília Mendonça (*Sentimento Louco*), project collaborations with top musical acts like Maiara & Maraisa, and high-profile scripted thrillers including *Obsessão*, *Quem Matou PC Farias*, and *Tiros no Escuro*. Notably, the platform has secured veteran Brazilian TV director Boninho to helming the creative direction for the new reality series *Oi, Sumido*.

Meanwhile, regional tentpoles elsewhere include the Argentine feature *Barreda*, the third season of the globally recognized Colombian franchise revival *Betty La Fea, Más Fea Que Nunca*, and the high-octane Mexican legal thriller *El Juicio*, featuring stars Eugenio Derbez and Pedro Alonso.

### Live Sports: The Ultimate Anti-Churn Retention Engine

While scripted content builds brand loyalty, Amazon is betting heavily on live sports rights as its primary acquisition and anti-churn mechanism. Data presented by Prime Video revealed that **over 20 million Latin American households** consumed sports content on the platform in 2026 alone—a metric surpassing the total sports viewership recorded across the entire preceding year.

A foundational pillar of this sports push is an 11-year global partnership with the NBA, expanding coverage in Brazil and Mexico to over **200 live games per season**, while introducing live NBA broadcasts to Argentina, Colombia, and Chile for the first time. In Brazilian soccer, Prime Video’s broadcast hours for the *Copa do Brasil* have more than doubled since launch, complemented by rights for the top-tier *Série A* domestic league.

In Mexico, Amazon secured an exclusive four-year agreement starting late September 2026 to broadcast 38 home matches of the Mexican National Football Team, establishing Prime Video as a primary destination for national sports fandom.

### The Super-App Aggregator Play: Channels Expansion

Beyond content, Amazon is executing an aggressive infrastructure expansion aimed at turning Prime Video into the central hub for streaming in Latin America. Consumers in six additional markets—Costa Rica, the Dominican Republic, Guatemala, Paraguay, and Peru—will gain access to **Prime Video Channels**, allowing users to subscribe directly to third-party streaming partners within the unified Prime Video interface.

In addition, digital movie purchases and rentals (TVOD) are launching across these five nations as well as Argentina. Crucially, these features operate independently of a core Amazon Prime membership, permitting standalone engagement with the platform's storefront. This move effectively positions Amazon as a primary media aggregator, monetizing third-party subscriptions and digital transactions through shared billing and sign-on infrastructure.

### Building Audiovisual Moats Through Local Workforce Development

To sustain a multi-billion-dollar production pipeline, Amazon is simultaneously funding localized technical training and creative education programs. In Brazil, the third edition of the *Brasil no Set* initiative—run in partnership with BRAVI and ICAB—is expanding training into the North and Northeast regions, alongside alliances with federal and municipal institutes.

In Mexico, Amazon introduced professional workshops in collaboration with *By Invitation Only* and regional acting academies, offering full scholarships for underrepresented talent. A newly inked partnership with the SAE Institute will focus specifically on physical production and post-production engineering, building a scalable ecosystem of specialized labor to supply Amazon’s growing studio needs.

### Strategic Analysis: The Emerging Market Playbook

Amazon’s $2 billion Latin American strategy marks a critical maturation phase in streaming dynamics. As North American and European subscription markets face saturation, streaming platforms must look to Latin America's tech-savvy, mobile-first population of over 650 million people.

By unifying local culturally tailored scripted hits, unscripted reality formats, premium live sports, third-party distribution channels, and regional workforce development, Amazon is building an integrated entertainment moat. For competitors like Netflix, Disney, and Warner Bros. Discovery, the message is unequivocal: winning emerging markets requires deep regional capital commitment, structural local partnerships, and an ecosystem model that goes far beyond simple video distribution.
